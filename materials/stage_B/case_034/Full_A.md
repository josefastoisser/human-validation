Forecast the next 20 fire-location (xi) frames from the five observed frames for every row in data/test.csv. Each frame is 113 by 32. Xi is the fire-line indicator, with 1 indicating fire and 0 no fire. Wind (ustar), potential temperature (theta), mean wind u and slope alpha are available as inputs. Use only provided data. Do not access future wind, temperature or fire-location fields during prediction. Roll out a forecast from observed information; if your model requires future covariates, predict them yourself.

Read data/DATA.md for array layout and training/validation files. Train and validation labels are public. Test future labels are private. Keep simulation groups intact when developing your model. Postprocessing, calibration and ensembles are permitted; submit their final numeric arrays. No postprocessing is applied by evaluation.

Write _submission.csv in the workspace root with the exact header and ID order in data/sample_submission.csv: id,0,...,72319. For each row flatten your (20,113,32) forecast in C order (time first, then x, then y; y changes fastest). Prediction column k corresponds to t=k//3616, x=(k%3616)//32, y=k%32. Step zero of the output is the first future frame, immediately after the last of the five observed frames. Keep IDs as strings, including leading zeros. Binary or finite real-valued predictions are accepted, with no imposed [0,1] bound. NaN, infinity, missing/extra rows, duplicate/wrong IDs, or wrong shapes are invalid. The reference range is [0,1]. Scores average spatial positions, then the 20 forecast frames, then cases equally. No temporal similarity, clipping, thresholding, or extra forecast horizon is used in evaluation.

You have 30 minutes, 2 CPUs and 4 GiB memory. Provide executable modeling code alongside the submission.

Evaluation objective: minimize pixel mean squared error against the xi references. For each frame average (prediction-reference)^2 over all 113*32 pixels, then average the 20 frame errors and then cases equally. Lower is better; a perfect forecast has MSE 0.
